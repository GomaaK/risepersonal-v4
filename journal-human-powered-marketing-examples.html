Journal · Human-powered marketing

Human-powered marketing, with practical examples.

The idea is simple: people trust people. The practice is a set of small, repeatable habits that put a real person in front of the right audience.

By Rise Personal · Last reviewed 30 October 2026

The short answer

Human-powered marketing is a way of marketing a company through the visible reputation of the people who lead it and speak for it, rather than through logos or product claims alone. In practice it means choosing a few people, defining what each stands for, and giving them a steady, approved way to share real expertise. It works when the people are willing and the company supports them.

The idea in one paragraph

Buyers, partners and candidates trust people more easily than they trust brands. A logo can be redrawn in an afternoon. A person's record, voice and judgement take years to build and cannot be copied. Human-powered marketing starts from that observation and plans around it.

It does not mean dropping advertising, data or tools. It means that what those tools carry is a real person with something specific to say, and that the audience can tell.

Five illustrative examples

These are illustrations of the method, not descriptions of any client's work. Each one is small enough to start without a campaign.

  • The explaining specialist. A senior engineer writes a short plain note each time a rule or standard changes, saying what it means for a customer. The sales team forwards it. The company becomes the place people ask.
  • The forwardable introduction. A founder writes a one-screen note that anyone who knows them can send to a potential partner. Introductions arrive clearer, and the founder does not repeat the same story.
  • The small roundtable. A practice lead hosts a private conversation for a few peers on one subject, under agreed ground rules. People remember who convened it.
  • The named team page. Instead of a stock photo grid, each person lists what they handle and how to reach them. Customers know whom to call, and candidates see who they would work with.
  • The leader's byline. When the company has news, the leader writes a short signed piece on what it means for customers, rather than only publishing an announcement in the company voice.

How to start with two people

Do not begin with everyone. Most companies need a few visible people done well, not everyone done thinly. Choose two people whose judgement customers already rely on and who are willing to be seen.

  • Define what each person stands for in two sentences, and three subjects where they are credible.
  • Agree what they may and may not say, and who approves it.
  • Give each a steady format they can sustain: a short note, a talk, a roundtable, a profile page.
  • Record every conversation that begins because of their work, and who they spoke to.
  • Review after a set period with the people, not only the numbers, and adjust.

Where it goes wrong

The first failure is choosing people who do not want to be visible. Visibility that is performed under pressure reads as such. Start with willing people and let others join when they see it working.

The second is approval friction. If every post needs a long chain of sign-offs, the rhythm dies. Agree the rules once, in advance, and review them rarely.

The third is dependence on one person. If a company's reputation sits on one leader and that person leaves, so does the trust. Spread visibility across a small group and keep the company's own record strong.

How to know it is working

Follower counts are a weak measure here. Better signals are conversations: prospects who mention a person by name, introductions that arrive pre-warmed, candidates who say they read someone's writing, and invitations to speak. Keep a simple log and read it with the team.

We do not promise followers, rankings or revenue from this work. We promise a clear plan, written scope and your approval at every step.

A worked illustration

Imagine a small advisory firm whose buyers are finance directors. The firm's marketing today is a brochure and a logo. Two partners are well regarded by clients but have no public record.

Under a human-powered plan, each partner chooses three subjects, such as how a rule is applied in practice or how to prepare a board for a decision. Each writes one short signed note every so often, hosts a small private roundtable now and then, and has a profile page that lists what they handle. The firm's website shows who to call. Enquiries start to mention a partner by name, and the partners spend less time re-explaining who they are. This is an illustration of the method, not a result we are claiming for any client.

Questions to ask before you start

  • Which two people would our buyers most like to hear from?
  • What do those people know that others in our sector do not explain well?
  • What will the company let them say, and who decides?
  • What happens to the plan if one of them leaves?

A one page audit you can do alone

Write down every person who speaks for your company in public. Then answer these questions for each.

  • What is this person known for, in one sentence?
  • Where can a stranger find evidence of it?
  • Does the way they describe themselves match how others describe them?
  • Are they willing, and do they have time?
  • What is the next small thing they could publish or host?

Questions people ask

Is human-powered marketing the same as influencer marketing?

No. Influencer marketing usually pays an outside person to speak for a brand. Human-powered marketing builds the visible reputation of the people who already work for or lead the company.

Does it replace advertising?

No. It changes what advertising carries. A real person with something specific to say usually makes every other channel work harder.

Which companies suit it?

Companies whose buyers decide on trust, such as professional services, healthcare, technology and advisory. It suits any business where people ask who is behind the company.

Can it work for a small team?

Yes. Two or three visible people with a steady, approved format are often enough to begin.
