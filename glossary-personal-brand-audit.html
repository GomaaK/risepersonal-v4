Personal brand audit: a plain definition.

A structured look at the gap between how you want to be seen and how you are.

By Rise Personal · Last reviewed 30 October 2026

The short answer

A personal brand audit is a structured check of what people find and say about you, compared with what you intend to be known for. It reviews search results, profiles, biographies and how others describe you, and ends with a short list of gaps to fix in order.

The definition

An audit measures the distance between your intended position and the evidence. It is a review, not a score of your worth.

Steps you can take alone

  • Search your name in a private window, with and without your city and profession.
  • List every profile and biography that describes you, and compare wording.
  • Ask three people for three words that describe you, unprompted.
  • Compare the results with the sentence you would use to introduce yourself.
  • Order the gaps by how much they cost you.

Where it is used

People run an audit before a job change, a fundraise, a board appointment, a speaking season or a new market. It is most useful when something is about to be read by strangers, because it shows what they will find before they find it.

What the result looks like

A good audit ends with a short list. It might say that your biography differs in three places, your profile does not state who you help, an old article describes a former role, and two colleagues use words you did not expect. You then fix the cheapest and most visible gaps first and leave the rest for later.

What an audit should not do

  • Score you against other people.
  • Promise a ranking or a result.
  • Store or share your information without your agreement.
  • Replace a conversation with the people who know you.

Questions people ask

Can I audit my own brand?

Yes. The steps above need no tools.

How often should I audit?

When your role changes or before an important meeting.
